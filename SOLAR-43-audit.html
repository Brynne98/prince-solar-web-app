What the audit found

  1. The size comes from three raw logs, not from features. Per-inverter readings (513 bytes a row, one row per inverter per minute), string readings (2 rows per inverter per minute) and inverter history are 80% of the database.
  2. String readings are kept 90 to 120 days but read back 15 minutes at most. Only the Live screen (newest row) and the phone alerts (last 15 min) read them.
  3. Per-inverter readings are kept 90 to 120 days but read back 30 days at most. Most readers want the newest row or the last 72 hours. The 30-day reader is the grid feed-scale check in sync-plant-energy.
  4. The weekly “downsample strings” job does nothing useful, and it fails. It thins the same 90-day-old months that the drop job deletes 30 minutes later. Its one run this week stopped at the 120 s limit, and no run has ever recorded a success.
  5. Minute history (agg_minute) is the only data kept forever, at full minute detail. The day chart for past dates draws 5-minute steps, so after the recent window, 4 of every 5 rows are never shown.
  6. Inverter history stores every upload (about 850 a day per inverter) and keeps it 120 days. The charts draw 5-minute steps (288 a day) and go back 60 days.
  7. Two background readers take most of the database time. Since 5 Aug, api_balance used 7.3 h and api_alerts_due 5.6 h. The alerts call comes from the TODO app every ~7.5 min, takes 1.7 s each time and runs the balance check inside. This bears on SOLAR-42, the memory swapping.
  8. The app counts every stored row on each load. api_db_stats averages 1.8 s, and its worst call took 55 s, just to work out how many days of history exist.
  9. Features overlap a lot, but cheaply. The four optional pages repeat the Live period tiles and day chart, and the Trends 30-day bars. They share the same calls, so they cost almost nothing in storage. Duplication is a design question, not a storage one.

What it means for the free plan

  • About 450 MB is usable once the fixed 15 MB and some headroom are taken off.
  • Today's rules: about 2 one-inverter homes in year one, and fewer each year after.
  • Proposed: about 9 homes in year one and 5 by year three.
  • A 3-inverter home costs about 2.5× a one-inverter home for raw readings.

How to get there

  • Store readings and strings in day-sized tables, not month-sized ones. A month-sized table can only be dropped once its last day is old enough, so a 2- or 7-day limit would still keep a month or more.
  • Roll minutes older than 120 days into 5-minute rows in a separate table. The day chart reads whichever table covers the date.
  • Write inverter history as 5-minute maximums, the way the chart already draws it.

The “kept forever” promise

  • Sign-in screen: “Minute-by-minute solar history, kept forever.” (auth.jsx:110)
  • Terms: “keeps a permanent history of it” (legal.jsx:15).
  • Under the plan, history is still kept forever, but in 5-minute steps after 120 days. A wording that stays true: “Your solar history, minute by minute, kept for good.” Minute detail would cover the recent four months only.

Paid tier options

Options only. The choice is yours.

A · Free keeps everything in the plan

  • Everything above is free.
  • Paid adds things that aren't storage: phone alerts, more than one plant, CSV export.
  • Simplest promise. Storage cost grows with every free home.

B · Free has a time window

  • Free: live data, 120 days of day charts, and daily and monthly totals forever.
  • Paid: 5-minute day charts forever, plus inverter and grid-supply history past 60 days.
  • Free costs about 37 MB a home, and that stops growing after 120 days.

C · Paid covers the bigger plan

  • The Supabase Pro plan (check current pricing) moves the cap from 0.5 GB to several GB.
  • At 48 MB a home-year, a few paying homes would carry many free ones.
  • Needs either A or B anyway, so growth stays predictable.
