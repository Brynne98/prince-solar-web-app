# Outage of 2026-09-18, and the string "check" light

Captured live at 16:45 SAST while the grid was still down, because the readings
would be ambiguous afterwards. Two separate findings came out of it: the check
light is a false alarm, and the dead-grid question that migration `0017` left
open is now answered by measurement.

Plant **538820** (two inverters, master `2508290475`, slave `2512082438`).
All times SAST. Nothing here has been changed in code — this is a record only.

---

## 1. The "check" light is an empty MPPT input, not a fault

The badge comes from one line, [public/tabs.jsx:906](public/tabs.jsx:906):

```js
const dead = s.v < 1.5 && s.p < 5, idle = s.p < 5;
```

No daylight guard, no outage guard, no memory of whether the string has ever
produced. It is a bare threshold on the latest sample.

**String input 1 on both inverters has never produced.** 30 days of history,
every sample:

| day | inverter | string | max W | max V |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09-18 | 2508290475 | 1 | 0.0 | 4.6 |
| 09-18 | 2512082438 | 1 | 0.0 | 5.1 |
| 09-17 → 08-19 | both | 1 | 0.0 | 4.6 – 5.5 |

Voltage wanders between 0.4 V and 5.5 V day and night — at midnight, at solar
noon, in an outage, identically. That is a floating unconnected MPPT input, not
a string with a problem. String 2 on each inverter is the real one (peaks today
4029 W and 3069 W).

**Why only one shows "check".** The float drifts across the 1.5 V line, so which
inverter trips the badge changes through the day. Today, out of 988 samples:

| inverter | string | samples | V min | V max | max W | samples that show "check" | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2508290475 | 1 | 988 | 0.60 | 4.60 | 0.0 | 543 | 55.0 |
| 2508290475 | 2 | 988 | 16.20 | 258.40 | 4029.0 | 0 | 0.0 |
| 2512082438 | 1 | 987 | 0.40 | 5.10 | 0.0 | 349 | 35.4 |
| 2512082438 | 2 | 987 | 16.90 | 286.50 | 3069.0 | 0 | 0.0 |

At 09:00–15:30 it was mostly the slave showing "check" (1.7–1.9 V while the
master sat at 4.4–4.6 V). By 16:30 they had swapped. The badge flickers between
two inputs that are both simply not there.

### Tested against the outage directly

Queried after Brynne raised it: the badge is not caused by the power being off.
Over 8 days, samples showing `check` on string 1, split by grid state:

| day | dead-grid samples | shows check | of those, grid UP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 09-18 | 62 | 570 | 516 |
| 09-17 | 0 | 792 | 792 |
| 09-16 | 0 | 818 | 818 |
| 09-15 | 0 | 796 | 796 |
| 09-14 | 0 | 643 | 643 |
| 09-13 | 0 | 635 | 635 |
| 09-12 | 0 | 636 | 636 |
| 09-11 | 0 | 739 | 739 |

(master `2508290475`; slave same shape, and today 349 check samples with none
during the outage.) Seven of those days had no outage at all and the badge still
showed on ~55% of samples. It is on about half of every day, which is why it
reads as intermittent.

**Not outage-related at all.** The earlier theory — battery full, inverter
curtailing, MPPT shut down — is wrong here: the battery was at 20% and
*discharging* when the badge was showing.

### If this gets fixed later

Options, cheapest first, none applied:

- Don't badge a string with no lifetime production. `strings.today_kwh` is 0 for
  every string on this plant, so it cannot be the test as it stands — needs a
  real lifetime total, or a "has this input ever exceeded N W" lookback.
- Hide inputs that have never produced, rather than badge them. A 2-string
  inverter showing 2 strings is more honest than 2 real + 2 ghosts.
- At minimum, require daylight and another string producing before "check" —
  which is already the condition on the hint line below the cards
  (`anyDead && a.pvNow > 500`), just not on the badge itself.

Related: plant **495944** (the parents', 15-string inverter) shows 13 of 15
inputs at 0 V — the same ghost-input problem, thirteen times over.

## 2. What a dead grid actually reads — the 0017 question, answered

This is the sample that `grid-relay-open-sample` said was still missing. The
2026-09-04 11:15 minute turned out to be the reconnect-delay window (grid back,
relay still open). **Today's is the genuine dead-grid interval.**

Onset: master device_time **16:08:46**, still down at 16:45.

| | relay closed (16:08) | relay open, grid dead (16:09 →) |
|---|---|---|
| `grid_volt_v` | 240.6 | **5.3 – 15.2** |
| `grid_freq_hz` | 49.91 | **0** |
| `grid_relay_status` | `1` | **`0`** |
| `grid_w` | 21 | **0** |
| `output_volt_v` | 240.7 | **229.9 – 230.2** |
| `output_freq_hz` | 49.91 | **50.00** |

**A dead grid reads a few volts, not zero, and 0 Hz.** The residual 5–15 V is
sensor float on the disconnected side, and it differs per inverter (master runs
7.8–15.2 V, slave sits flat at 5.4 V).

Consequences for the code as written:

- `q_grid_present()` = `bool_or(grid_volt_v > 100)` — **confirmed correct.** A
  dead grid reads under 16 V, nowhere near 100 V. The premise of
  `0015_grid_presence.sql`, untested until now, holds.
- `grid_relay_status = '0'` + `grid_volt_v < 100` together are the confirmed
  dead-grid signature. Relay alone is not specific (0017 notes deliberate
  relay-open stretches when self-powered); voltage alone is now enough.
- Output pinned to 230.0 V / 50.00 Hz confirms islanding, same as 09-04.

### The burst sampler worked

`0029_grid_burst.sql` fired as designed: first burst row **16:09:03**, 17 s
after the inverter's own dead sample, then 10 s spacing. By 16:44 it held 180
rows per inverter for this outage — which is essentially the whole `grid_burst`
table (358 rows all-time before today's tail).

Two things follow:

- Its job is done. The open question it existed to answer is answered, so the
  burst block in `poll` and the table can be removed per Brynne's 2026-09-05
  decision, once this finding is written into FEATURES.md.
- Until then it keeps writing 12 rows/min for as long as the grid is out. A
  multi-hour outage will put thousands of rows in a table meant to be temporary.

## Re-running any of this

Read-only, against production:

```bash
supabase db query --linked -o table "<sql>"
```

`ts` on `readings`, `strings`, `agg_minute` and `grid_burst` is **epoch seconds**,
not milliseconds. Convert with `to_timestamp(ts) at time zone 'Africa/Johannesburg'`.

Today's per-string history:

```sql
select to_char(to_timestamp(ts) at time zone 'Africa/Johannesburg','HH24:MI') t,
       sn, no, volt_v, current_a, power_w
from strings
where plant_id = 538820
  and ts >= extract(epoch from date_trunc('day', now() at time zone 'Africa/Johannesburg') at time zone 'Africa/Johannesburg')::bigint
order by ts, sn, no;
```

The outage window in `readings` and `grid_burst`:

```sql
select * from readings   where plant_id = 538820 and ts >= 1789740540 order by ts, sn;
select * from grid_burst where plant_id = 538820 and ts >= 1789740540 order by ts, sn;
```

(`1789740540` = the 16:09:00 poll, the first row carrying `grid_relay_status = '0'`. The
inverter's own `device_time` on that row is 16:08:46 — the poll minute and the device
clock differ by up to a minute throughout.)

## 4. Decision: deferred 2026-09-18, waiting for more data

Brynne chose "no panels" as the label for inputs that have never produced, then
stopped short of building it. Nothing below is implemented. Recorded so the
investigation does not have to be repeated.

### What the console said (Grok and Fable; Astra's usage limit was spent)

Both landed on the same mechanism: a **cumulative** per-input peak, latched in
`poll_commit` from the rows already staged in `_st`, joined by primary key in
`api_overview`. Not a lookback — `strings` partitions are dropped at 90 days
(`0036_drop_old_partitions.sql`), so only a running latch can answer "ever".

They split on the column. Grok: peak **voltage**, since a real string in shade
reads 0 W at hundreds of volts. Fable: voltage alone **cannot** separate an empty
input from a snapped conductor, both near 0 V — so `max_w >= 5 or max_v >= 20`.
The split does not need settling: a cumulative latch covers both cases, so store
both columns.

Fable also warned that an inverter swap or firmware renumber restarts the key
`(plant_id, sn, no)` and would relabel every input, so the label needs ~24 h of
samples before it may be asserted — mirroring `n < 30` in `plant_features_detect`.

### Verified against production, not taken on trust

- **The `string_dead` alert shares the same 1.5 V test** (`0055_grid_presence_freshness.sql:177`)
  and is already firing. Measured over 7 days:
  - plant **538820**: **zero** — its float sits at ~4.4 V at midday, above the line,
    and the alert is gated to hours 11–14 with a sibling over 200 W.
  - plant **495944** (parents'): **13 inputs per inverter, 240 samples, every day.**
    That plant's unused inputs read a true 0 V rather than a float. It is sending a
    daily "13 solar strings look dead" digest for panels that do not exist, and will
    keep doing so until this is fixed.
- **No shortcut in the API.** `pvIV[]` carries only `pvNo, vpv, ipv, ppv, todayPv`
  (`API.md:133`), and `todayPv` is 0 for strings that made 4 kW today. `mpptIV[]` is
  empty on this hardware. There is no "connected" flag to read.
- **An inline lookback is not viable**: `max(power_w)` over 2 days measured 1.5 s
  (538820) and 1.1 s (495944) on the snapshot RPC, which the app polls.

### The shape it would take

1. `private.string_peaks(plant_id, sn, no, max_w, max_v, samples, first_ts, last_ts)`,
   backfilled once, then one grouped upsert in `poll_commit` beside the strings insert.
2. `api_overview` adds `everProduced` per string: false only when
   `max_w < 5 and max_v < 20` and `samples >= 1440`.
3. The same gate on `api_alerts_due_raw`'s `dead_held`, or 495944 keeps paging.
4. `public/data.jsx` passes it through; `public/tabs.jsx` shows "no panels", and the
   badge picks up the daylight + sibling gate the hint line already uses.

### Open risks, unresolved

- It redefines `poll_commit`, the live write path.
- "No panels" asserts as fact something only the owner can confirm. A string
  mis-wired at install has never produced and would be hidden by the label. An
  owner override was raised and not adopted; nobody has asked for one.

## 5. The 20% floor, and the grid-charge ceiling

Raised by Brynne at 17:15 while the grid was still down: the inverters were
below the 20% floor she had set, and looked to her like they had lost track of
it. They had not. Two separate facts came out of chasing it, and the second one
is the actionable one.

### The floor is a grid-takeover point, not a hard stop

The 20% was honoured exactly, and the readings show it to the minute
(master `2508290475`, all times SAST):

| time | SOC | `batt_w` | `grid_w` | `load_w` | `grid_volt_v` | relay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15:45 | 21 | −2364 | 21 | 2635 | 241.4 | 1 |
| **15:50** | **20** | **−7** | **2105** | 2529 | 235.5 | 1 |
| 15:55 | 20 | −10 | 2131 | 2543 | 236.0 | 1 |
| 16:00 | 20 | −14 | 2042 | 2527 | 236.0 | 1 |
| 16:05 | 20 | +339 | 21 | 240 | 239.8 | 1 |
| **16:10** | 21 | +213 | 0 | 342 | **14.4** | **0** |

At 15:50 the battery went to zero and the grid picked up 2.1 kW within one
minute. **SOC then held flat at exactly 20% for fifteen minutes.** That is the
setting working, not failing.

It only went below after the grid died at **16:08:46**. Islanded, the inverter's
choice is to discharge past the floor or to cut the power to the house — the
floor has no meaning with no grid to fall back to. Over 16:30 → 17:18, 49
samples: SOC 20 → 19, master averaging −141 W against a 216 W load. One percent
in fifty minutes, because the big loads were already off.

**Two different settings, and this is the distinction that caused the
confusion:**

- **Timeslot SOC%** (System Mode → Use Timer) — the grid-takeover point. Brynne's
  20%. Binds only while the grid is alive.
- **Shutdown %** (Battery Setup) — the real off-grid floor, where the inverter
  stops. Brynne believes hers is **10%**; not confirmed, see the caveat below.

Grid returned **17:19** (voltage back at 241.0 V with the relay still open — the
reconnect delay), relay closed **17:20**. Outage length **71 minutes**.

### Grid charging is capped at ~2 kW per inverter, and the battery is not the limit

Measured at 17:33, both inverters charging from the grid:

| | `batt_w` | `batt_current_a` | `batt_voltage_v` | `grid_w` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| master `2508290475` | 2054 | −38.7 | 53.10 | 2144 |
| slave `2512082438` | 2043 | −38.4 | 53.20 | 2311 |

4.1 kW into the battery, 4.45 kW off the grid. Both inverters pinned flat at
38.7 A. Thirty days of history splits charging by source and settles what is
doing the capping:

| inverter | max charge **from solar** | max charge **from grid** |
|---|---|---|
| `2508290475` | **4724 W / 85.9 A** | 2059 W / 39.0 A |
| `2512082438` | **3948 W / 71.7 A** | 2043 W / 38.5 A |

The bank takes 86 A from solar and 39 A from the grid. **Neither the battery nor
the inverter is the constraint** — it is a grid-charge-specific setting, and the
same figure on both inverters says it is configuration, not hardware. The likely
place is the **Power (W)** column on the timeslot that has *Grid Charge* ticked,
where 2000 W is a common default; failing that, Battery Setup → Max Charge
Current.

### Bank size, derived twice

Not recorded anywhere, so it was measured two ways and they agree:

- **Discharging** 14:50 → 15:45: 3.45 kWh out of the pair for a 12.5% drop →
  **0.276 kWh per percent**.
- **Charging** 17:26 → 17:33: 1% per ~4 min at 4.1 kW → **0.273 kWh per percent**.

So roughly **27.5 kWh** across the two inverters. At the capped 4.1 kW, 21% →
full is about 5¼ hours; uncapped it would be under three.

### The caveat that matters for the app

**None of the settings above are readable from here.** `_shared/sunsynk.ts` calls
telemetry endpoints only — there is no `/common/setting/{sn}/read` in the poller,
so the app cannot know the configured floor, the Shutdown %, or the grid-charge
cap. Anything the app says about a ceiling has to be inferred from observed
history (the 30-day split above), and must be worded as observation, not as the
setting.
